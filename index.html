What is Collaborative Divorce?

A structured legal process in which both spouses and their attorneys commit in writing to resolve all issues outside of court — with the support of financial and mental health professionals working for both of you.

No Courtroom

All decisions are made at the negotiating table — not by a judge. You and your spouse maintain control over every outcome for your family.

Full Transparency

All parties commit to honest, complete financial disclosure — eliminating costly discovery battles and building real trust in the process.

Children First

A trained child specialist joins the team to ensure your children's needs are centered in every decision — protecting them from adult conflict.

Less Costly

Without prolonged litigation, collaborative cases resolve faster — at a fraction of the time and expense of a contested divorce trial.

Client Portal

E-Signatures

How the secure electronic signature process works for your client documents.

When your attorney sends you a document to sign, you receive a notification to log into your secure client portal. From there, you can review the full document before signing anything. Once you've read it, you draw your signature directly on screen and click "Sign." That's it — no printing, no scanning, no email attachments.

Yes. Texas adopted the Uniform Electronic Transactions Act (UETA), which gives electronic signatures the same legal weight as a handwritten signature. An e-signature is enforceable in Texas courts as long as the signer intended to sign and had the opportunity to review what they were signing. Both conditions are built into how this portal works.

You are always shown the complete document before your signature is requested. You cannot sign without first having access to the full file. There are no blank forms, no pre-signed pages, and no surprises.

Every signature event is recorded with:

  • Your full name and the exact document you signed
  • The date and time of signing (Central Time)
  • Your IP address and browser information
  • A SHA-256 cryptographic fingerprint of the document taken at the moment of signing

A certificate page containing all of this information — including an image of your drawn signature — is permanently appended to the signed document and stored securely. This record cannot be altered after the fact.

The cryptographic hash taken at signing proves the document was not modified after you signed it. If the document had been changed in any way — even by a single character — the hash would not match. This gives your signed document stronger tamper-evidence than a traditional paper signature.

For most agreements, yes. The portal supports attorney counter-signature, meaning your attorney reviews the client-signed document and adds their own signature before the document is considered fully executed. Both signatures, with their separate timestamps and verification data, appear on the final certificate.

All documents are stored in encrypted cloud storage dedicated exclusively to your firm. No document data is shared with third parties. The signed PDF and its audit record are retained in accordance with Texas State Bar guidelines on client file retention.

FAQ

Common questions about working with Savage Family Law.

Collaborative divorce typically costs significantly less than traditional litigation because it avoids the expense of prolonged court proceedings, discovery battles, and multiple hearings. Costs vary depending on the complexity of your case — assets involved, custody arrangements, and how much agreement already exists between the parties. We discuss fees and structure transparently at your initial consultation.

Most collaborative cases resolve in four to eight months. Compare that to traditional contested divorce, which commonly takes one to two years or longer. The timeline depends on the parties' availability, the complexity of financial and custody issues, and how quickly both sides can reach agreement — but the process is designed to be time-limited from the start.

Collaborative divorce requires both parties to agree to the process. If your spouse is hesitant, an initial conversation with their own attorney about what collaboration actually involves — versus what they may fear about it — often changes the picture. If collaboration genuinely isn't possible, we can discuss other options including mediation or traditional representation.
